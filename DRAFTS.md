# Drafts — Everyday Things, Two Lines

Judge brief (in order): (1) really explains one everyday thing to a 10-year-old
in one vivid sentence + a second sentence on the mapping/limits; (2) the
analogy is concrete and surprising — things a 10-year-old can see, hold or
stand in, joined in a way not met before; (3) line 1 is ONE sentence, no list
of comparisons, no second metaphor crowding it; (4) line 2 is honest and
specific about what the analogy maps and what it leaves out, not praise;
(5) the two lines work as a pair, line 2 explaining line 1, in language a
10-year-old follows without being talked down to. A vivid picture that leaves
a wrong idea is worse than a plain one that holds.

## Candidate A — rain / sponge (CHOSEN)

> Rain is a cloud wringing itself out like a wet sponge: once it has soaked up so many tiny drops that they are too heavy to float, it lets them fall.
> The sponge part fits — both hold water until they are full and then drip — but nobody squeezes a cloud, and a sponge drinks from a puddle while a cloud grows its drops out of thin air.

## Candidate B — queue / train

> A queue is a human train: each person is a carriage hooked to the one in front, and the whole line only moves as fast as the engine at the front.
> It maps how one slow person at the front sets the pace for everyone behind, but leaves out that carriages can't step out of line, overtake, or get bored and wander off.

## Candidate C — toaster / sunbed

> A toaster is a tiny sunbed for bread: two glowing red walls hug each slice until it turns golden brown, then pop it up when it is done tanning.
> The sunbed part fits — both brown things with steady heat — but the sun tans you slowly with light, while a toaster cooks with red-hot wires that can burn the bread black in seconds.

## Candidate D — traffic jam / river and dam

> A traffic jam is a river of cars that hit a dam nobody built: hundreds of drivers all push forward, but the water has nowhere to go.
> It maps how cars pile up behind one narrow spot like water behind a dam, but leaves out that drivers can turn off down another street, and water cannot choose at all.

## Pairwise reasoning

- **A vs B:** both on-topic. On criterion 2, a cloud as a sponge is more
  surprising than a queue as a train (a familiar comparison), and a sponge is
  something a 10-year-old can hold and squeeze. A also explains a mechanism
  (why rain falls) that a child genuinely wonders about. A wins.
- **A vs C:** a sunbed is less familiar to a 10-year-old than a sponge, and
  "done tanning" crowds a second image into line 1. A wins.
- **A vs D:** "river of cars" is a cliché, and "a dam nobody built" confuses
  rather than clarifies — it risks leaving the wrong idea about what causes a
  jam. A wins.

## Why A holds up

- Line 1 is a single sentence with a single metaphor (sponge), and it is
  physically honest: cloud droplets really do grow until they are too heavy
  to stay suspended.
- Line 2 names the mapping (hold water until full, then drip) and two
  specific limits (nobody squeezes a cloud; a sponge drinks from a puddle,
  a cloud builds drops from vapour) instead of praising the analogy.
- Revision notes: an earlier draft of line 1 said the cloud "soaks up
  invisible water", which was misleading — clouds do not soak water up from
  below, so the wording was replaced with drops growing too heavy to float,
  and line 2 was rewritten to carry the correction explicitly.
